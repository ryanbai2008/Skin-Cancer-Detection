About This Project

The Skin Disease Classifier (SDC) is a deep learning-based system designed to assist with early detection of common skin diseases. It uses a convolutional neural network trained on dermatological images to classify seven conditions, including melanoma and basal cell carcinoma.

Disclaimer: This tool is intended for a preliminary diagnosis to help you get a better understanding of the situation. Please consult a medical professional as soon as possible if you believe you are at risk of any disease, regardless of the results from this test.

Common Symptoms

Actinic Keratosis / Intra-epithelial Carcinoma ▼

Actinic Keratosis often appears as a rough patch on the skin, and on parts of the skin that are commonly exposed to sunlight. They pose a risk of developing into Intra-epithelial Carcinoma, which Squaumous Cell Carcinoma is a form of. The two diseases appear in very similar forms, so it is crucial to get professional diagnosis and treatment from a dermatologist to determine the disease's severity if either form is suspected. According to the NIH, signs of inflammation, rapid growth, bleeding, or ulceration may suggest need of biopsy.

Basal Cell Carcinoma ▼

Basal Cell Carcinoma often appears as a slightly transparent bump on the skin, and on parts of the skin that are commonly exposed to sunlight. It may also appear as a browm bump with a rolled border. If this disease is suspected, it is crucial to get professional diagnosis and treatment from a healthcare professional.

Benign Keratosis-like Lesion ▼

Benign Keratosis-like Lesions (Seborrheic Keratosis) often appears as a waxy and raised portion of the skin, as a brown, black, or light tan color. This is a type of noncancerous/benign skin growth. They are not contaigous, however some symptoms include itchiness. If bleeding or rapid growth occurs, it may be signs of skin cancer and it is crucial to consult and/or get treatment from a healthcare professional.

Dermatofibroma ▼

Dermatofibroma (or Cellular Dermatofibroma) often appears as a brown, mole-like bump. They commonly appear on areas like the legs, and are a noncancerous/benign skin growth. They are usually less than one centimeter in diameter. Healthcare providers can diagnose this through a skin biopsy, and treatment is able to remove the growth.

Melanocytic Nevus ▼

Melanocytic Nevus (or Moles) often appears as a dark spot on the skin which varies in size. The moles are typically harmless, however a large amount of moles may lead to an increased risk of developing Melanoma.

Vascular Lesion ▼

Vascular Lesions often appear with skin discoloration like red or purple marks. Symptoms may include pain, swelling, bleeding, and skin discoloration. If a Vascular Lesion is suspected, it is crucial to get professional diagnosis and treatment.

Melanoma ▼

Melanoma often appears as a change in an existing mole or the development of an pigmented loooking growth on the skin. It often appears on areas of the skin that are exposed to sunlight. Look for moles with an asymmetrical shape, several different colors, moles larger than 0.25 inches in diameter, or bleeding. If Melanoma is suspected, please consult a medical professional for diagnosis and treatment.
